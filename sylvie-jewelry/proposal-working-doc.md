## DECISIONS MADE SO FAR (our walkthrough)

1. **Why they left the old agency:** strong "thought-leader" *positioning* but (a) execution gap — they
   handed Sylvie work Sylvie couldn't execute (couldn't find/manage writers), and (b) Josh's read is the
   expertise wasn't actually that strong either. Premium positioning, thin delivery.
2. **Positioning for the proposal:** compete on *visible, hands-on doing* — "partner, not agency."
   Let proof talk (the live Portland audit > any credentials). Don't attack her; let contrast land.
   Address Gary's "classic agency model / spread too thin" fear directly — propose dedicated/embedded capacity.
3. **Content delivery sits with Top Click (Option 3 → 2):** Josh sources AND manages the writer through the
   test phase (transparently, billed at cost + small management line, so it doesn't contradict the
   "no white-label" stance). Prove pages ship, then hand steady-state to Sylvie's choice (employ in-house or
   keep via Top Click). Rationale: the deal lives or dies on closing the execution gap; content is the most
   likely workflow to stall, so Top Click must own it. Writer ~R25k/month.
4. **SEO framing:** Sylvie has real authority (Ahrefs DR 50 / SEMrush AS 41) but pointed at the wrong targets
   — informational/birthstone traffic that doesn't convert, and head terms ("engagement rings", ~300k/mo,
   ~$12k value) a B2B brand structurally can't win vs B2C shops. Frame the opportunity as **redeploying
   existing DR-50 authority** toward business-relevant terms. Keep birthstone content but stop investing there.
5. **Strategic spine = the Gabriel & Co. model.** Sylvie's growth lever is NOT more B2C sales; it's
   **retailer acquisition (400 → 800)** + **retailer retention/enablement**. Win retailers by being the
   supplier that ALSO brings shoppers to their store → become indispensable ("we become their agency as well
   as their supplier of jewelry"). Two audiences, kept distinct:
   - **B2C demand** (consumers asking local jewelers for Sylvie) — fuels retention + acquisition.
   - **B2B acquisition** (reaching jeweler owners directly) — LinkedIn / custom-audience play.
6. **Lead with engine (a) retailer-enablement / local SEO, NOT (b) direct B2B owner-acquisition.** Proposed
   phasing (this is the backbone — confirm in AM):
   - **Phase 1 (test phase):** On Sylvie's OWN site — technical cleanup + redeploy DR-50 authority + rebuild
     a batch of local retailer landing pages properly (Portland as worked example). Zero retailer risk.
     Goal: prove execution + move rankings.
   - **Phase 2:** Take the proven result to ONE friendly pilot retailer (Des Moines / Portland type), co-op
     funded, replicate on a live account.
   - **Phase 3:** Productize into the retailer-ACQUISITION pitch ("join Sylvie, we market your store") AND
     layer in direct B2B owner-targeting (LinkedIn / custom audiences). Engine (b) switches on here, backed
     by proof.
   Logic: (a) is the only engine that can be proven safely + fast, AND its proof is the ammunition for (b).
